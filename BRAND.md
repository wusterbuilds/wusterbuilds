# Wuster Builds visual system

The system takes inspiration from industrial product interfaces: functional grids, technical labels, generous whitespace, direct typography, and small high-energy color fields. It does not reuse Teenage Engineering trademarks, fonts, layouts, or product artwork.

## Principles

1. **Instrument, not decoration.** Every visual element should label, organize, demonstrate, or indicate state.
2. **One loud move.** Use one dominant product promise and one high-energy accent per surface.
3. **Hard geometry.** Prefer rules, grids, rectangular modules, and square controls over soft card stacks.
4. **Technical warmth.** Pair precise labels and monospace metadata with plain, human copy.
5. **Product first.** Replace abstract hero art with a real product demonstration as soon as one is available.

## Type

- Product names: bold grotesk/sans fallback, tight tracking.
- Body copy: neutral system sans.
- Metadata and state labels: monospace, uppercase, increased tracking.
- Avoid decorative display faces and badge walls.

## Layout

- Use a 12-column base grid with 2 px rules on large brand surfaces.
- Keep the product promise in the left 60–65 percent.
- Use the right rail for process, state, metrics, or a real product frame.
- Prefer zero-radius containers; reserve subtle rounding for interactive product UI.
- Social previews use a 2:1 canvas and the same modular hierarchy as README heroes.

## Product accents

- **Plumb for Excel:** orange; cell coordinates, assumptions, and trace states.
- **Plumb Platform:** lime; sequential workflow and review gates.
- **Incenta:** blue; program modules and quantified benefit.
- **Nabo:** its established orange/brown palette; the four-stage execution loop.

## Voice

- Lead with the outcome.
- Use short sentences and concrete nouns.
- State project maturity and safety boundaries visibly.
- Never imply that a prototype is production-ready or that generated professional conclusions are authoritative.
